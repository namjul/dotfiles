The seed prompt was accompanied by three primers, condensed here:

### Primer: generative sequence (Christopher Alexander, via iamronen)

Wholeness is a measure of the interrelatedness of centers; it increases when a system unfolds gradually through structure-preserving transformations and decreases when parts are made separately and assembled. Planning-then-assembling is tossing all coins at once; a generative sequence tosses one coin at a time in a correct order, greatly improving the odds that the transformations lead to wholeness. Alexander's Fundamental Process: observe what exists, feel the weakness, formulate a small transformation that taps its latent potential, implement, reassess by felt experience, keep or discard, repeat.

### Primer: pockets of reducibility (Wolfram, via Ryan Singer)

Computational irreducibility means no shortcut exists — the only way to know the outcome is to run the process step by step. "Claudable" tasks sit in pockets of reducibility: enough structure, recurring patterns, and checkable criteria that a model can jump ahead. Judgment-heavy tasks (taste, values, novel context, trade-offs under uncertainty) tend to sit outside those pockets; their core resists reduction and must be deliberated step by step by the human.

### Primer: biomarkers

A biomarker is a little movement in the body-mind that indicates a phenomenal state with surgical precision where language is too coarse. Biomarkers discriminate similar-seeming internal states, signal satisfaction or incompleteness, and — when strong and well-known — constitute intuition. They are the human's instrument for tasting whether a task is reducible or irreducible.
